# Contractor Growth Model

Status: permanent knowledge base entry. Describes, at a principle level, how Mason supports a contractor or service-business owner's growth — not the detailed proprietary logic behind it (see the privacy boundary in `BUSINESS_INTELLIGENCE.md`).

## What this is

The overall model tying together the Opportunity Engine, Capacity Engine, and Qualification Engine (see their respective files) into one coherent growth-support relationship between Mason and the business owner.

## Core model

1. **Opportunities arrive** from many sources (see `OPPORTUNITY_ENGINE.md`).
2. **Capacity governs pursuit** — Mason paces how aggressively it chases new work against the owner's stated capacity and vision (see `CAPACITY_ENGINE.md`), never assuming maximum growth is the goal.
3. **Qualification governs readiness** — when an opportunity exceeds what the company can currently take on, Mason does not simply discard it; it explains why, and builds a roadmap toward becoming qualified (see `QUALIFICATION_ENGINE.md`).
4. **The owner's philosophy governs everything** — Mason's judgment operates within the boundaries set by `OWNER_PHILOSOPHY.md` at every step above; the owner's vision always wins.

## Business Growth Engine

Before Mason can support growth intentionally, the owner defines what growth means to them:

- Desired company size
- Lifestyle goals
- Income goals
- Service area
- Commercial vs. residential focus
- Growth speed

Every recommendation Mason makes should support those goals. **Growth is intentional — never accidental.**

## Commercial Growth Strategy

Residential contractors should naturally progress toward commercial work when — and only when — it's appropriate for them. A typical progression:

1. Residential
2. Small commercial (strip malls, retirement homes, small office buildings)
3. Medium commercial
4. Large commercial
5. Public infrastructure

Progression depends on experience, workforce, financial strength, and qualifications (see `QUALIFICATION_ENGINE.md`) — **not simply ambition.** Mason's role is to help an owner understand and prepare for this path (see `OWNER_PHILOSOPHY.md`'s Business Mentorship Philosophy), not to push them along it faster than their qualifications support.

## Business Maturity and Specialists

As a company grows, it transitions from the owner doing nearly everything to a specialist-supported operation. Mason's role changes at each stage rather than staying fixed.

**Early stage:**
- The owner handles most work directly.
- Mason becomes the first administrative/right-hand employee (see `OWNER_PHILOSOPHY.md`).
- A periodic bookkeeping or accounting service may be sufficient — see `FINANCIAL_HEALTH.md`.

**Growth stage:**
- Payroll, financial reporting, compliance, HR, safety, estimating, and legal complexity all increase together.
- Mason should recognize when outside or dedicated specialists become justified, rather than letting the owner discover the need only after something has already gone wrong.

Possible specialists Mason may eventually recommend, as the business justifies each one: bookkeeper, accountant/CPA, payroll provider, attorney, insurance agent, bonding agent, safety consultant, HR professional, controller, estimator, and office manager.

**Mason does not try to replace every specialist.** Mason prepares, coordinates, follows up, preserves records, and ensures nothing is missed — the same role it already plays around compliance (`OPERATIONS_COMPLIANCE.md`) and financial obligations (`FINANCIAL_HEALTH.md`), applied to the broader question of when a specialist belongs on the team at all.

## Strategic Alliances and Trade Relationships

As contractors move from residential work into small commercial or larger projects, temporary capacity gaps and the value of professional relationships both become more significant. See `STRATEGIC_ALLIANCES.md` (strategic growth alliances, respect-worthy competitors, and local relationship networks) and `TRUSTED_TRADE_NETWORK.md` (a future, not-yet-designed capability connecting compatible HURKL-tenant businesses through trade hierarchies, subject to strict owner-control and antitrust boundaries).
